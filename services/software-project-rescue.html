When recovery becomes necessary

The software may still run. Confidence around it may not.

Rescue work often starts before a system fails completely. The common signal is that nobody can make a safe, evidence-backed promise about what happens next.

Delivery keeps slipping

Milestones move, estimates grow and working software is difficult to demonstrate.

Knowledge sits with one person

Builds, releases or incident recovery depend on an individual who may no longer be available.

Production changes feel unsafe

The product is live, but weak tests, unclear dependencies or fragile deployments make every release a risk.

Supplier confidence has broken down

The organisation cannot reconcile progress, spend, technical risk and the remaining route to value.

Ownership is unclear

Repositories, cloud accounts, domains, data, credentials or third-party services are not under reliable control.

Legacy systems block change

Useful business behaviour is trapped inside tightly coupled applications that are expensive to understand or modify.

Why this experience matters in a rescue

Inherited systems rarely arrive as a clean architecture problem. They combine live users, legacy behaviour, incomplete documentation, operational dependencies and commercial pressure.

A staged modernisation route protects useful behaviour while creating better boundaries around the parts that need to change. It gives the business decision points, reduces the blast radius of each intervention and avoids replacing working software merely to create the appearance of a fresh start.

FAQ

Project rescue, answered plainly

Can you take over software you did not build?

Yes. The first step is to establish access, ownership, reproducibility, operational evidence and the risks around the business-critical journeys before making delivery promises.

Do you always recommend a rewrite?

No. A rescue assessment compares keeping, repairing, replacing in stages and rebuilding. The recommendation follows the available evidence and operational constraints.

What do you need to begin?

An initial inventory of repositories, environments, hosting, deployments, known incidents, product priorities and the people who understand the critical workflows is enough to begin scoping access.

Can you work with the current supplier or internal team?

Yes. A controlled handover is often safer than an abrupt change. DuniaOps can work with existing engineers and suppliers while responsibilities, access and operational knowledge are transferred.

What happens after the assessment?

You receive prioritised findings and a staged recovery route. DuniaOps can deliver the agreed work, support your team or hand the evidence and plan to another supplier.

Is the initial Project Rescue conversation free?

Yes. The initial qualification call is free. If a structured assessment is the right next step, we agree its scope and commercial terms with you before beginning the paid assessment.

Need control before committing more budget?

Describe the system, the immediate risk and what has made the current route difficult to trust. We normally reply within two working days.
