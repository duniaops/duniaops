**Asteroids come in very different sizes.** Some are only a few metres across; others measure hundreds of kilometres. When we ask how big one is, we usually mean its **diameter**: roughly how wide the body is, not how close it will pass Earth. Many asteroids are irregular rocks rather than perfect balls, so a single diameter is a useful approximation.

## Put metres beside familiar things

Imagine a *hypothetical* 12-metre asteroid beside a bus about 12 metres long. A 100-metre asteroid would stretch roughly the length of a football pitch; an international pitch is usually 100–110 metres long. A 1-kilometre asteroid is 1,000 metres across—about ten 100-metre pitches end to end. These are length comparisons, not pictures of a real visitor or claims that every bus and pitch has exactly those dimensions.

Try drawing a line on paper with **1 centimetre representing 10 metres**. The 12-metre example needs 1.2 centimetres; the 100-metre example needs 10 centimetres. The 1-kilometre example would need a whole metre of paper. That is why a phone screen cannot show all three at one faithful scale.

## How do scientists know a size?

Some asteroids have been measured more directly, but many size figures are **estimates**. Astronomers can combine an asteroid's brightness with an assumption about how reflective its surface is. A dark rock and a bright rock can look equally bright while having different diameters. NASA/JPL's size estimator therefore shows ranges, not a promise of an exact width. A later observation may change the estimate.

## Rockimals' playful size ladder

In the reviewed 1.5.1 app source, Rockimals uses the **upper end of a visitor's estimated diameter** to choose its physical size band and default animal. The boundaries below are in metres; each upper boundary belongs to the *next* band. An unassigned visitor at exactly 8 metres, for example, defaults to Tavi rather than Niko. A visitor assigned a story character can display a different animal while keeping its factual size band.

| Estimated diameter used by the game | Animal friend | Playful size label |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under 8 m | Niko the mouse | Car |
| 8 to under 20 m | Tavi the rabbit | Bus |
| 20 to under 50 m | Barney the fox | House |
| 50 to under 120 m | Kito the tiger | Plane |

| 300 to under 800 m | Bobo the elephant | Stadium |

| 2,000 m or more | Enoli the whale | Mountain |

The labels are friendly *categories*, not exact dimensions of a car, building or mountain. For instance, our 100-metre example is roughly one pitch long in ordinary speech, yet the default animal for the game's 100-metre band is **Kito**, not the “football pitch” Pofi band. The character art and cover are not scaled models of the asteroids.
